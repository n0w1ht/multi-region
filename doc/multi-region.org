# The source of the manual that C-h R multi-region shows.  After
# editing it, run `make info' to rebuild multi-region.texi and
# multi-region.info.

Emacs normally lets you select one piece of text at a time.
multi-region lets you select several.  In "I love ClaudeAI very much"
you can have /love/ and /much/ selected, and nothing else, then copy
both with one key.

It works in text, Org, Markdown and code buffers, in read-only buffers
such as eww, in EPUB books (nov.el), and in PDFs (pdf-tools or
pdf-canvas).  In a PDF you can select passages on several pages and copy
them in one go.

Open this manual with ~C-h R multi-region~ or ~C-c M h~.  In it, ~i~
looks up a command or option by name and ~s~ searches the text.

* Getting started

** Turning it on
#+findex: multi-region-mode
#+findex: global-multi-region-mode

Put the folder of multi-region on your load path and add this to your
init file:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(require 'multi-region)
(global-multi-region-mode 1)
#+end_src

To try it in one buffer only, use ~M-x multi-region-mode~ instead.

The mode line shows =MR= while the mode is on, and =MR[3]= when three
pieces are selected.

** A first try

1. Open any text.  Hold ~Control~ and drag the mouse over a word.  It
   stays highlighted.
2. ~C-drag~ over another word, further on.  Both are highlighted.
3. Press ~M-w~.  Both words are copied, one per line, and deselected.
4. Changed your mind?  ~C-c M /~ selects them again.

That is most of it.  The rest of this manual is details.

* Selecting

** With the mouse
#+findex: multi-region-mouse-drag

| Mouse            | What it does                                                 |
|------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------|
| ~C-drag~         | Add the dragged text to the selections                       |
| ~C-double-click~ | Add the word under the pointer                               |
| ~C-triple-click~ | Add the line under the pointer                               |
| ~C-click~        | Deselect the selection under the pointer, or select a picture |

You can start the usual way: drag, or ~C-SPC~ and move.  As soon as
you ~C-drag~ somewhere else, that first region becomes a selection too.
So "drag, then C-drag, then C-drag" works as in a word processor.

Selections that touch or overlap become one.

** With the keyboard
#+findex: multi-region-toggle
#+vindex: multi-region-thing

~C-c M SPC~ does what makes sense at point:

- with an active region, it selects the region;
- inside a selection, it deselects it;
- otherwise, it selects the picture or the word at point.

The "word" can be something else: see =multi-region-thing= in [[*Options][Options]].

* Using the selections

All commands are under the prefix ~C-c M~.  This manual uses that
key; if you prefer another, see [[*Other keys][Other keys]].

| Key         | What it does                                         |
|-------------+------------------------------------------------------|
| ~C-c M w~   | Copy all selections as one piece of text (also ~M-w~) |
| ~C-c M k~   | Kill all selections (also ~C-w~)                      |
| ~C-c M r~   | Replace every selection with the same text           |
| ~C-c M x~   | Run a region command on every selection              |
| ~C-c M n~   | Jump to the next selection                           |
| ~C-c M p~   | Jump to the previous selection                       |
| ~C-c M u~   | Deselect the most recent selection                   |
| ~C-c M c~   | Deselect everything                                  |
| ~C-c M /~   | Bring back what was last deselected all at once      |
| ~C-c M h~   | Open this manual                                     |

** Copying and killing
#+findex: multi-region-copy
#+findex: multi-region-kill
#+findex: multi-region-kill-ring-save
#+findex: multi-region-kill-region
#+vindex: multi-region-separator
#+vindex: multi-region-keep-after-copy

~M-w~ and ~C-w~ act on the selections while there are any, and behave
as usual when there are none.  You don't have to think about it.

Copied pieces are joined with a newline.  For a different separator just
this once, use ~C-u C-c M w~ and type it.  To change it for good, set
=multi-region-separator=.

Copying deselects everything, so you can start fresh.  Set
=multi-region-keep-after-copy= to keep the selections instead.

A word that a PDF split with a =¬= is joined again: "inflam¬ mation"
is copied as "inflammation".

** Changing all selections at once
#+findex: multi-region-replace
#+findex: multi-region-do

~C-c M r~ asks for a text and puts it in place of every selection.

~C-c M x~ asks for a command and runs it on each selection as if it were
the region.  Good ones to try: =upcase-region=, =comment-region=,
=fill-region=.

~C-/~ (undo) puts the text back after either, and after ~C-w~.

** Moving between selections
#+findex: multi-region-next
#+findex: multi-region-previous

~C-c M n~ and ~C-c M p~ move point to the next and previous selection.

** Deselecting and bringing back
#+findex: multi-region-remove-last
#+findex: multi-region-clear
#+findex: multi-region-restore
#+findex: multi-region-keyboard-quit

~C-c M u~ deselects the selection you made last.  ~C-c M c~ deselects
everything.

~C-c M /~ brings back what was last deselected all at once, whether by
~C-c M c~, by copying, or by ~C-g~ in a PDF or EPUB.  Selections you
made since are kept.

* Pictures
#+vindex: multi-region-image-margin

A picture in the buffer is selected whole or not at all.  Drag across it
with the text around it, or ~C-click~ it to select it alone.  A selected
picture gets a band in the selection color; =multi-region-image-margin=
sets its width, and 0 turns it off.

This works for pictures in eww and EPUBs, and for inline images in Org
(~C-c C-x C-v~) and Markdown (~C-c C-x C-i~).  In Org and Markdown, a link
to a picture file counts even when the picture isn't shown.

Copying takes only the text.  The pictures are for packages that build
notes from your selections, such as readq; see [[*For other packages][For other packages]].

* In each kind of buffer

** Text, Org, Markdown and code

Everything in this manual works.

** eww and other read-only buffers

Selecting, copying, jumping and deselecting work.  Killing, replacing and
running a command stop with "Buffer is read-only", as they would for an
ordinary region (killing copies first).

On a link, ~C-drag~ selects.  A ~C-click~ that selects nothing still
opens the link in a new window, as usual.

** EPUB books

In books opened with nov.el, selections are remembered for each
chapter and come back when you return to it.  Copying collects them
from all chapters, in reading order.  ~C-g~ deselects everything in every chapter.

** PDFs (pdf-tools)
#+findex: multi-region-pdf-mouse-set-region
#+findex: multi-region-pdf-mouse-rectangle

A PDF page is a picture, so there is no point to move: you select with
the mouse.

| Mouse     | What it does                                     |
|-----------+--------------------------------------------------|
| drag      | Start over with a new selection on the page      |
| ~C-drag~  | Add another selection                            |
| click     | Nothing: the selections stay                     |
| ~M-drag~  | Select a picture area, such as a figure           |
| ~M-click~ | Deselect the picture area under the pointer       |

Selections are remembered for each page, and copying collects them from
all pages.  ~C-g~ deselects everything on every page.

Copy, deselect last, deselect all and bring back work as in text.
Kill, replace, run-command and next/previous don't apply; ~C-w~ copies
instead of killing.

Highlights follow =pdf-view-selection-style=, so what you see is
exactly what is copied.

** PDFs (pdf-canvas)

pdf-canvas keeps several selections itself, on any number of pages
(drag, then ~C-drag~; ~M-drag~ for a picture area).  multi-region gives
them the same commands as in a pdf-tools buffer, and ~C-g~ remembers
what it deselects, so ~C-c M /~ can bring it back.

* Options

Change these with ~M-x customize-group RET multi-region~, or with
=setq= in your init file.

- =multi-region-separator= :: What goes between copied pieces.  A
  newline by default.
- =multi-region-keep-after-copy= :: Keep the selections after copying.
  Off by default.
- =multi-region-thing= :: What ~C-c M SPC~ selects at point: =word= (the
  default), =symbol=, =sentence=, =line=, or anything =thing-at-point=
  knows.
- =multi-region-image-margin= :: Width in pixels of the band around
  selected pictures.  4 by default; 0 for none.
- =multi-region-prefix-key= :: The key that leads to the commands.
  ~C-c M~ by default; see [[*Other keys][Other keys]].
- =multi-region-face= :: How selections look.  Like the region, by
  default.

** Other keys
#+vindex: multi-region-prefix-key
#+vindex: multi-region-mode-map
#+vindex: multi-region-command-map

To use ~C-c s~ as the prefix instead of ~C-c M~:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setopt multi-region-prefix-key "C-c s")
#+end_src

=setopt= needs Emacs 29.  On older versions, use ~M-x customize-option
RET multi-region-prefix-key~, or =setq= it /before/ multi-region is
loaded; a =setq= afterwards does nothing.  Set it to =nil= for no
prefix at all, and bind the commands in =multi-region-command-map= to
keys of your own.

To select with ~M-S-drag~ instead of ~C-drag~:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(define-key multi-region-mode-map [C-down-mouse-1] nil)
(define-key multi-region-mode-map [M-S-down-mouse-1] #'multi-region-mouse-drag)
#+end_src

* For other packages
#+findex: multi-region-selections

=(multi-region-selections &optional absorb)= returns the selections of
the current buffer in reading order, without changing them.  With
=absorb=, an active region becomes a selection first.  Each selection is
a property list:

- in text: =(:beg BEG :end END :text TEXT)=;
- in an EPUB, also =:chapter=, the chapter number;
- with pictures, also =:images=, each =(:beg BEG :end END :file FILE)=,
  or =:data= and =:type= for a picture that is not in a file;
- in a PDF: =(:page PAGE :edges (LEFT TOP RIGHT BOTTOM) :text TEXT)=,
  edges as fractions of the page.  A picture area also has =:figure t=
  and empty text.

=(multi-region-clear)= deselects everything.

* When pdf-tools is updated

multi-region relies on a few details of how pdf-tools works inside.
They are all in one file, =multi-region-pdf-tools.el=, listed at its
top.  After updating pdf-tools, run this in multi-region's folder:

#+begin_src sh
make test-pdf-tools
#+end_src

If something no longer holds, the test says which, for example "breaks
assumption 2 in multi-region-pdf-tools.el".  Until it is fixed, PDF
selections may not draw or copy correctly; text and EPUB buffers are
not affected.
